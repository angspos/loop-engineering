Where Actor · Critic · Reviser lives in my workflow

Untested — and it is the least-watched step in the loop

Nothing has ever checked the Reviser. Nine agents were interviewed, re-interviewed on their failed gaps, and audited mechanically. The consolidation step has had none of that — yet it is where /panel step 3 says "merge duplicates, surface disagreements under their own heading, do not soften, drop the noise." Every one of those is a place a blocker can quietly become a concern, or a live disagreement can get resolved silently instead of being put to you in two lines.

It is also the step with the worst incentive: the orchestrator that consolidates is the same one that chose the question, launched the panel and will present the result. Merging is where a run can be made to look better than it was.

What would test it: take a past pass with a known disagreement, consolidate it twice, and check that the disagreement survives to the top of both. That is now loop-engineer's to measure — a Round 1 blocker that appears as a concern in the consolidated report, or a disagreement that appears only in one seat's section and not under its own heading.

Autonomy, dialed per seat

Andrew Ng — "different degrees to which systems can be agentic" · The Batch 253, 12 Jun 2024

The dial is not something to bolt on — it is already latent in the agent files. Some seats carry a fixed standard they check against; some exist to find what nobody wrote down. Reading the ten files, this is where each already sits.

The tenth seat — loop-engineer

The nine ask whether Fade Readers is any good. This one asks whether the reviewing is any good, and whether it earns its cost. Nothing else was looking at that — and one day's audit made the case on its own.

Caught by nothing

Three stale specs in a day

The payoff-art count, the curriculum ladder, the octopus color. Each individually findable. Nobody's job was to notice they were a pattern.

Caught by nothing

A number that was guessed

A reviewer asked for a count answered "approximately 48" against an actual 59 — while every quotation it gave was exact.

Caught by nothing

A fix pass that broke things

Repairing nine findings introduced two new ones. Only the re-run caught it. No product reviewer would ever file that.

What it measures — thresholds, not vibes

Evidence compliance under 90% · a seat filing the same non-zero count every pass · a Round 1 finding vanishing from Round 2 without an explicit concede · any spec claim the code contradicts · any number not traceable to a command · a re-raise missing the required "rejected on <date> because <reason>; re-raising because <what changed>" line · your gate agreement rate · report length against findings that changed a decision · --all run on a single-domain question.

Two guardrails, and they are the design

It never opens the app. Its inputs are wiki/feedback/panel/, lint-report.json, the agent files and the decision log. A process reviewer with opinions about letter colors has become a tenth product reviewer, which is worse than not existing.

It runs every fifth pass, not every pass. It measures cost; adding cost to every run would make it the thing it is checking. Most of its passes should end in "nothing found" — and a clean process pass is exactly what tells you the loop is safe to lean on.

It carries principles, not people. Verification bounds autonomy · compute, never estimate · decompose before adding autonomy · evals over intuition · read parallelises, writing collides. Deliberately unattributed: a reviewer that invents a position for a living author has done the exact thing this seat exists to catch.

Evals — the unglamorous version

Andrew Ng — evals and error analysis as the biggest predictor of progress · The Batch, 15 Oct 2025

Ng calls disciplined evaluation the single biggest predictor of whether an agentic system works. It doesn't start as infrastructure. It starts as a log.

From run 1

Log

Every gate decision: what the panel recommended, what you decided, one line on why they differed.

~ run 10

Count

Tally the recurring types. "SKEPTIC over-cuts." "CHILD misses bedtime context." Objective counting before any scoring.

Ongoing

Fix

Each recurring type becomes a rubric line, a lint rule, or a dial change. Designed out, not caught again.

Once stable

Judge

LLM-as-judge scores findings against the rubric, so low-value findings never reach your surface at all.

Your agreement rate with the panel is the number that unlocks the ladder below. Not a feeling that it's working — a rate you can point at.
